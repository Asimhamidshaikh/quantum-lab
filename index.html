1. The Schrödinger Wave Equation

Calculus & Partial Derivatives

The fundamental differential equation describing how the quantum state of a physical system changes over time:

$$\mathbf{i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \Psi(x,t) = \left[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} + V(x,t) \right] \Psi(x,t)}$$

$i$: Imaginary unit ($i^2 = -1$ giving wave phase oscillation).

$\hbar$: Reduced Planck constant ($h/2\pi$).

$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}$: Partial derivative with respect to time.

$\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}$: Second partial spatial derivative (Kinetic Energy operator).

2. Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

Fourier Transforms

Precision limits on complementary variables. Position ($x$) and Momentum ($p$) are Fourier conjugates:

$$\mathbf{\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}}$$

Compressing the wavepacket in spatial domain ($\Delta x \to 0$) causes the Fourier momentum spectrum ($\Delta p$) to broaden rapidly!

3. Dirac Bra-Ket Notation

Linear Algebra

State vectors live in complex Hilbert Space. Inner products represent probability amplitudes:

$$\mathbf{|\Psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle, \quad |\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1}$$

$| \Psi \rangle$ (Ket): Column vector of complex state amplitudes.

$\langle \Psi |$ (Bra): Conjugate transpose row vector.

4. Quantum Universality & Gates

Unitary Matrices

Any quantum computation can be synthesized via a universal set of unitary operators (Hadamard, CNOT, Phase):

$$\mathbf{H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad H|0\rangle = \frac{|0\rangle + |1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}}$$

Unitary condition $U^\dagger U = I$ guarantees conservation of probability during computation!

Module 1

The Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation

$$i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Psi(\mathbf{r},t) = \left[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + V(\mathbf{r},t) \right] \Psi(\mathbf{r},t)$$

This linear partial differential equation governs how the quantum state of a physical system changes with time. The Hamiltonian operator $\hat{H} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + V$ represents total energy (kinetic + potential).

Module 2

Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

$$\sigma_x \sigma_p \ge \frac{\hbar}{2} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad [\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$$

Derived from the non-commutativity of position and momentum operators. In Fourier transform terms, a localized spatial wave packet requires a broad range of momentum frequencies.

Module 3

Probability Density & Current Continuity

$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \mathbf{\nabla} \cdot \mathbf{J} = 0, \quad \mathbf{J} = \frac{\hbar}{2mi}\left(\Psi^* \mathbf{\nabla}\Psi - \Psi \mathbf{\nabla}\Psi^*\right)$$

Ensures global probability conservation $\int |\Psi|^2 dV = 1$. The probability flux density $\mathbf{J}$ tracks how wave probability flows through space.

Module 4

Quantum Entanglement & Bell State Matrix

$$|\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle), \quad E(\rho) = -\text{Tr}(\rho \log_2 \rho)$$

Entangled bipartite states cannot be factored into product states $|\psi_A\rangle \otimes |\psi_B\rangle$. von Neumann entropy quantifies degree of quantum non-locality.
